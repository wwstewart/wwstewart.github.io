What I worked on

Each entry opens to show what it can prove and what it cannot. Click Bond’s systems and data stay private, so this exhibit is my account of the work, in my words, with the tools named.

  • Vulnerabilities Information Security

    I partnered with the Information Security team to identify and patch software vulnerabilities using Tenable Nessus and CrowdStrike Falcon.

    Evidence
    Tools
    Tenable Nessus for scanning, CrowdStrike Falcon for the endpoints.
    Shown here
    Nothing from inside the company. Scan results name real systems, so they stay there.
    Ask me about
    How a scan finding becomes a patch, and how we decided what to patch first.

  • Support tickets Infrastructure

    I monitored hardware and software tickets in Ivanti and gave remote software support through ManageEngine Endpoint Central.

    Evidence
    Tools
    Ivanti for the queue, ManageEngine Endpoint Central for remote sessions.
    Shown here
    No tickets. They carry names and machine details.
    Ask me about
    A remote fix done while the person waited at a production machine, and what I check first on a slow computer.

  • Accounts and installs Infrastructure

    I managed user groups and permissions in Active Directory, and installed software and hardware across company computers.

    Evidence
    Tools
    Active Directory users and groups.
    Shown here
    No screenshots. A directory view is a map of the company.
    Ask me about
    Why group membership, not individual permissions, is how access should be granted, and what I learned from doing it the right way.

  • Campus network Networking

    I worked with Networking to troubleshoot switches, access points, and Ethernet ports around the campus.

    Evidence
    Tools
    The switches, access points, and cabling themselves; LogicMonitor for what the network reported.
    Shown here
    No diagrams. A network map is the one thing a manufacturer should never publish.
    Ask me about
    Tracing a dead port back through the patch panel to the switch, and how a production floor changes the order you do things in.

  • Software requests Apps and Data

    I worked with the Apps and Data team on software rationalization requests.

    Evidence
    What it is
    Deciding which of several tools that do the same job a company keeps, and what happens to the rest.
    Shown here
    No request records, since they name vendors and prices.
    Ask me about
    What a good request looked like, and why the answer was usually about licensing and support, not features.

  • AI tools Across teams

    I used AI tools to speed up technical documentation, ticket triage, and troubleshooting research on routine support tasks.

    Evidence
    Shown here
    The way I work with AI is the subject of Exhibit 06, with my real prompts.
    Where it stopped
    Nothing from the company’s systems went into an AI tool. The drafts did; the data did not.
    Ask me about
    Which tasks AI is good for on a help desk, and which ones I would not hand it.

Finding

What I learned

Manufacturing IT is different from office IT. Production can’t wait while you figure something out, and the person you’re helping is usually standing at a machine, not sitting at a desk.

I also moved from Ohio to Nevada for this internship, so relocating for a summer role is something I have already done.
