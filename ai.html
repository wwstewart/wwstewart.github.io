Why my prompts work

Starting the site

Portfolio, after the AI had looked into the design tools

“Let’s go ahead with this. I want you to build a prototype website that you believe suits my use case. Remember, I’m doing this for an IT/Cybersecurity/Infrastructure/Networking internship. Any IT field is fine. Additionally, refer to the writing guidelines I’ve given you.”

  1. Phrase 1 of 6

    “Let’s go ahead with this.”

    The go ahead. Nothing was built until I said so.

  2. Phrase 2 of 6

    “build a prototype website”

    Names the deliverable. The word prototype sets the bar: a first version to react to.

  3. Phrase 3 of 6

    “that you believe suits my use case”

    Hands the design to the AI, so it proposes a direction instead of asking me twenty questions.

  4. Phrase 4 of 6

    “I’m doing this for an IT/Cybersecurity/Infrastructure/Networking internship.”

    The audience. Every choice on this site traces back to this sentence.

  5. Phrase 5 of 6

    “Any IT field is fine.”

    A boundary wide enough to fit any IT posting I apply to.

  6. Phrase 6 of 6

    “refer to the writing guidelines I’ve given you.”

    Points to rules I wrote once (no hyphens, honest claims, my voice) instead of repeating them.

What came back

About 35 minutes later, a home page and an internship page written from my resume, checked for contrast, keyboard use, phone layouts, and dark mode.

Vetting outside tools

Portfolio, sharing a video about AI design tools

“Can you use the information from this reel to assist in building the website? [link] Let me know, don’t begin work quite yet”

  1. Phrase 1 of 2

    “Can you use the information from this reel”

    Brings in an outside source and asks whether it fits, instead of assuming it does.

  2. Phrase 2 of 2

    “Let me know, don’t begin work quite yet”

    A stop point. The AI researched and reported back, and nothing ran until I said go.

What came back

Instagram blocked the AI, and it said so instead of guessing. Once I named the three tools in the video, it warned that two of them have look alike copies on GitHub, used only the originals, and skipped an installer hook that would run a script every time a page file was edited.

Running work in parallel

Job search, five tailored applications at once

“Tailor both Resumes and Cover Letters to the following companies (more information on the google sheet, these are all jobs you suggested): [five postings] Remember to dispatch individual agents for each of these different resumes and cover letters. We’re on ultracode, so we can support several agents running in parallel. Have agents check the other’s work, as well. Use this github repo as reference, as well: [link] This github repo won’t be a standard, I just want to see how well it works in this session.”

  1. Phrase 1 of 6

    “Tailor both Resumes and Cover Letters”

    Two deliverables per posting, each written for that job.

  2. Phrase 2 of 6

    “more information on the google sheet”

    Points to my tracker as the one source of truth instead of pasting the details again.

  3. Phrase 3 of 6

    “dispatch individual agents for each”

    One AI agent per application, all working at the same time.

  4. Phrase 4 of 6

    “We’re on ultracode”

    A setting that lets the AI run many agents in parallel. I turn it on when the work splits cleanly.

  5. Phrase 5 of 6

    “Have agents check the other’s work”

    Built in review. Every draft was checked by a second agent before it reached me.

  6. Phrase 6 of 6

    “This github repo won’t be a standard, I just want to see how well it works”

    A trial, not a commitment. The agents judged it useful as a checklist only, so I didn’t adopt it.

What came back

Five tailored resumes and cover letters, each one page, each checked by another agent, plus a verdict on the trial repo: keep it as a checklist, not a standard.

Giving feedback

Portfolio, after the first prototype

“Looks awesome, but it needs to be unique, and incredibly interactive. I like the simplicity as it is, but I sent you those skills so we could make it really stand out. I want this to be as unique as you can make it, while still fitting the cybersecurity realm.”

  1. Phrase 1 of 4

    “it needs to be unique, and incredibly interactive”

    The gap, stated as an outcome instead of a list of features.

  2. Phrase 2 of 4

    “I like the simplicity as it is”

    Says what to keep, so the next version doesn’t throw out what worked.

  3. Phrase 3 of 4

    “I sent you those skills so we could make it really stand out”

    Ties the ask to the tools already in play.

  4. Phrase 4 of 4

    “while still fitting the cybersecurity realm”

    A guardrail: bold, but on topic for security hiring.

What came back

Three directions to choose from, each with its tradeoffs, then the version you’re looking at: a live security check on the home page, an Inspect mode on every page, and a work map that traces routes.

Asking for the next step

Job search, after my first two applications

“Write tailored follow up letters and include who they should be routed to”

  1. Phrase 1 of 2

    “tailored follow up letters”

    The deliverable, written for each company.

  2. Phrase 2 of 2

    “include who they should be routed to”

    Asks for the logistics too, so the letters arrive ready to send.

What came back

A letter for each application, addressed to the best contact with backups and how to reach them, and every name checked against the page it came from.

My job search, run with AI

Most of my AI work this fall went into finding an internship. This is the system we built, from the first search to the follow up. Select a stage to see who does it and the prompt behind it.

  1. Stage 1 of 8

    Plan

    Who
    Both, the AI drafted it and I set the limits
    What it does
    A written search plan built from my resume: target roles, where to look, a timeline, and a weekly routine.
    Prompt

    “Let’s create a plan to assist me in my job search.”

  2. Stage 2 of 8

    Rules

    Who
    Me
    What it does
    My cover letter guidelines: one template, about 350 words, no hyphens, and nothing I couldn’t back up in an interview. Every letter starts from them.
    Prompt

    “Just added it to our library, Cover_Letter_Guidelines.md”

  3. Stage 3 of 8

    Digest

    Who
    AI
    What it does
    Every Monday and Thursday morning, a scheduled search for new postings that fit my schedule, my minimum pay, and the terms I’m open to.
    Prompt

    “The digest can run twice a week.”

  4. Stage 4 of 8

    Verify

    Who
    AI
    What it does
    Each posting is confirmed open on the employer’s own site before it reaches me. One run made about 230 searches, and 16 postings passed both checks.
    Instead of a prompt
    The AI built this check into the digest itself.

  5. Stage 5 of 8

    Track

    Who
    Both, the AI built it and I keep it current
    What it does
    A Google Sheet with one row per job, a status for each, a follow up date seven days after I apply, and a weekly goal.
    Prompt

    “Here’s our document for the job search. I’ve also installed the claude extension on Google Sheets, so feel free to use that”

  6. Stage 6 of 8

    Tailor

    Who
    AI, two agents per posting
    What it does
    One agent writes a resume and cover letter for the posting from my template and rules. A second agent checks the draft against the posting and my facts.
    Prompt

    “Remember to dispatch individual agents for each of these different resumes and cover letters. [...] Have agents check the other’s work, as well.”

  7. Stage 7 of 8

    Review

    Who
    Me
    What it does
    I read every draft, answer the questions only I can answer, and decide what gets sent. The AI never applies for me.
    Instead of a prompt
    This step stays with me.

  8. Stage 8 of 8

    Follow up

    Who
    Both, the AI drafts and I send
    What it does
    A follow up letter for each application, addressed to the best contact, with every name checked against the page it came from.
    Prompt

    “Write tailored follow up letters and include who they should be routed to”

AI output

  • Postings are confirmed at the source.

    A job counts only if it’s open on the employer’s own site.

  • Facts live in one place.

    My resume and rules are shared files, so one correction reaches every draft.

  • Drafts get a second reader.

    A separate agent checks each application before I see it.

  • I decide what’s public.

    My phone number stays off this site, and drafts leave out anything I want kept private.

  • I sign off.

    The AI drafts, I decide what gets sent, and it never applies for me.

What this means for your team

AI is part of how IT work gets done now. Here’s what I bring to it.

  • Clear requests

    A good prompt and a good ticket need the same things: the goal, the context, the limits, and what done looks like.

  • Checked results

    I make AI show its sources and check its own work, and I check the parts that matter before anything goes out.

  • Security first

    I vet tools before running them, the same way I’d treat any download on a work machine.

  • Real use at work

    At Click Bond I used AI tools to speed up technical documentation, ticket triage, and troubleshooting research on routine support tasks.

  • Work at scale

    When a job splits cleanly, I run it in parallel and build review into the plan, like my applications and this site.
