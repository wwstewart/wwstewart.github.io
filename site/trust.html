How it was built

Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, wrote the code and the copy from my prompts. I set the goal, the facts, and the rules, and I made the final calls. Exhibit 06 shows the prompts.

Before each version went up it was checked in a headless browser: no console errors, no policy violations, keyboard focus on every control, text contrast at WCAG AA or better, and no sideways scrolling at five screen widths.

Inspect mode, the switch in the header, shows the same measurements live on every part of every page.

Your visit

The Your visit button in the corner is a small security operations view of your own session: page load, the seal, the live check, where you scrolled, tab switches, copies, prints, keyboard use, opened drawers. It gives each event the rule that noticed it and a severity that never rises above low.

It is kept in memory in this tab, never sent, and Wipe clears it. There is no server to send it to; the Network tab of your developer tools shows that no request leaves.

With scripts on, this seal is drawn from the eleven signals listed here. With scripts off, it is the file’s own seal.

Your seal

What draws the seal

Eleven signals your browser gives every site it visits, read, joined, hashed, and turned into the ticks, arcs, and mark of the seal. All of it happens in your browser, and none of it leaves.

These are the same signals a tracker uses to recognize a browser without a cookie. Here they draw a picture and are then forgotten. Nothing is stored, nothing is sent, and reloading the page reads them again from scratch.

Not read: your address, your location, your history, anything you typed, or anything outside this tab. Firefox, Safari, and Brave hide or blur some of the signals above on purpose. If your seal looks plain, that is why, and it is a good thing.

The hash is SHA 256 where the browser offers it. The code is in assets/casefile.js, in plain text.
